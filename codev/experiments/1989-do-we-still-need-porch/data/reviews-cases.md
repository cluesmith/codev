**Interpretations used (fixed before counting; they apply notes.md, they do not change it):**
- `S-consult-loop` counts only a **porch-run round at iteration ≥2**, meaning porch iterated after a REQUEST_CHANGES, that found a real defect which was then fixed. First-round findings are not counted, because a builder without porch also runs one round. Each phase in each file gets one row. A row is `borderline` when the record does not say which round found what, or that porch (and not the architect) ran the round.
- `S-gate`: **every row is `borderline`.** Each records a human finding a real defect, or redirecting the design, while porch held a dev-, plan- or spec-approval gate. No review records porch refusing someone who was about to pass a gate.
- A force-advance at porch's 3-iteration ceiling that the record says left fixes unreviewed is `C-bypass` (F4). A force-advance the record says lost nothing is not counted, or appears only in a note.
- A pre-existing, flaky, load-sensitive or environmental test failure that blocked `porch done` is `C-check`. A misparsed or defaulted verdict is `C-text`.
- A defect in the `consult` tool, not porch, that reached the builder through porch's consultation step is at most a `borderline` `C-other` (F4).
- One incident recorded on several lines of a file is one row; the other lines go in the note. The same porch defect hitting different projects is a separate row per project, cross-referenced in the note.

**Observations from the sample that bear on F4:**
- 5 of 40 files record **no REQUEST_CHANGES from any working reviewer**: three COMMENT-only rounds (1789-plan-iter2, 403, 823-plan-iter2), one all-APPROVE with Gemini skipped (1658-phase_8), and one where the only REQUEST_CHANGES was a skipped Gemini lane that porch parsed as REQUEST_CHANGES (819). So "a rebuttal file exists" is not exactly "a round ended with a real REQUEST_CHANGES". The current `verdict.ts` treats COMMENT, and a missing VERDICT line, as approve (`allApprove`, lines 46–59). That was evidently not true when those older files were written.
- PIR single-pass rounds (1053, 1118, 1410): fixes are **not re-reviewed** by porch, and the rebuttal files say they go to the human at the `pr` gate instead.
- All 39 `fixed` files are one round's response. Whether a **later** porch round would have caught the same defect, or whether a builder running consults without porch would have caught it too, cannot be read from them. ## 6. What these records cannot show

- **The counterfactual.** No review records what would have happened without porch. Most save rows, every S-gate and every S-consult-loop, show that a gate hold or a later review round found something. They do not show that a builder without porch would have skipped that hold or round. notes.md already calls S-consult-loop weak evidence for porch.
- **Routine, unremarkable events.** Reviews are written by the builder at the end and record what was surprising. A check that failed on a real bug and was fixed in a minute, or a gate that simply held, is rarely written up. The zeros for S-check and S-consult-skip may be under-reporting, not absence. Costs are the more memorable events and may be over-represented.
- **Who ran a round, or who approved a gate.** Many rows are borderline because the review says "CMAP" or "approved" without saying whether porch or the architect drove it, or whether the approver was the human. Porch keeps `approved_at` but no `approved_by` (#1457, 1494:105), so gate provenance cannot be recovered from the review files.
- **Builds without porch.** No review states a soft-mode build. The pre-porch reviews are from an earlier tool set and protocol, so they are not a like-for-like comparison.
- **Base rates.** 105 of 285 reviews hold a case. A review with no case does not mean porch ran cleanly, only that nothing was written down. Per-function totals depend on how long and how candid each review is. Seven long reviews (1658, 1951, 1963, 1797, 1940, 1313, 1470) contribute 67 of the 234 rows.
- **Consistency across readers.** Six readers shared one brief and I reviewed every row, but clear-versus-borderline is a judgment. The quotes, file and line can be checked mechanically. The kind and certainty cannot.
- **Severity and cost in time.** Rows are counted, not weighted: an hour-long wedge and a 10-minute workaround count the same, and so do a security fix and a docs fix.
- **The rebuttal sample.** Rebuttal files show what the builder accepted in that round. They do not show whether the reviewer was right when it was rebutted. They also do not show whether the fixes were later reviewed: PIR single-pass and force-advanced phases were not.
